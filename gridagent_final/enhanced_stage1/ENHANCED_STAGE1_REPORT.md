## 3. Why Stage1 Is Necessary

Stage1 is the uncertainty entrance of the entire multi-stage framework.
Its purpose is not just to fit renewable time series, but to convert a long historical wind-PV trajectory into a small scenario set that can be passed downstream with explicit probabilities.

This stage is necessary for four reasons:

1. The downstream planning, dispatch, resilience, and risk modules cannot directly carry the full renewable uncertainty in raw hourly form without a sharp increase in computational burden.
2. Wind and PV uncertainty is joint, non-Gaussian, and time-dependent; using only point forecasts would hide the uncertainty structure that later stages must respond to.
3. The framework eventually needs a finite set of representative scenarios with probabilities, because later optimization and resilience assessment are scenario-driven rather than distribution-driven.
4. If Stage1 fails to preserve realistic renewable behavior, then later stages may optimize against unrealistic uncertainty and produce biased resilience conclusions.

In short, Stage1 is the compression-and-propagation layer for renewable uncertainty.

## 4. Current Method

The current method implemented in `conditional_wind_pv_uncertainty_modeling.py` is:

- Conditional Rolling DPGMM
- KMeans scenario reduction

The method logic is:

1. Read the Guangdong 2024 hourly wind and PV series.
2. Construct transition samples
   `[wind_(t-1), pv_(t-1), wind_t, pv_t]`.
3. Normalize wind and PV by their historical maxima.
4. Fit a local rolling Bayesian Gaussian mixture model on the 4D transition samples.
5. Generate annual scenarios sequentially, so the next hour is sampled conditionally on the previous generated hour.
6. Restore the original scale and apply practical output constraints:
   - nonnegative output,
   - upper clipping to historical maxima,
   - PV forced to zero at night/non-PV hours.
7. Cluster the sampled annual trajectories and retain 10 typical scenarios with empirical probabilities.

Compared with hour-wise independent sampling, the key enhancement is the transition-based conditional generation:

- original idea: sample `x_t` directly;
- current idea: sample `x_t` from `p(x_t | x_(t-1))`.

This makes the model explicitly time-coupled at the one-step level.

## 5. Why This Method Is a Reasonable Choice at This Stage

Based on the current project state, this method is a practical middle point between overly simple statistical sampling and data-hungry deep generative models.

### 5.1 It matches the available data scale

The current formal dataset is one year of hourly Guangdong data:

- 8784 hourly samples
- one wind series
- one PV series

This is enough to support local probabilistic modeling and annual scenario sampling, but still limited for training more complex deep generative models robustly.

### 5.2 It preserves multimodal and non-Gaussian uncertainty better than purely linear models

Wind and PV output distributions are strongly non-Gaussian, and PV additionally has a day-night structure with long zero-output periods.
DP-style mixture modeling is more flexible than single-Gaussian or linear residual assumptions for such data.

### 5.3 It directly targets short-term temporal continuity

The enhancement from 2D hourly modeling to 4D transition modeling is methodologically important.
It gives the model a mechanism to learn whether the next hour should remain high, remain low, rise, or fall, conditioned on the previously generated state.

### 5.4 It remains interpretable and operationally useful

The final output is not a black-box latent embedding.
It is a finite set of explicit yearly scenarios with scenario probabilities, which is exactly the form needed by later stochastic or resilience-oriented modules.

### 5.5 It is still computationally feasible

The current full run has already been completed successfully in the workspace.
Observed wall-clock time for the full-year run was about 3387 seconds, roughly 56.4 minutes, using:

- `window_radius = 24`
- `max_components = 8`
- `max_iter = 150`
- `n_sampled_scenarios = 200`
- `n_typical_scenarios = 10`

This is not cheap, but it is still practical for a formal dataset pipeline.

### 7.5 Main Interpretation of the Current Results

The current full-run results suggest the following.

#### Strengths

1. **The run completed successfully on the full 8784-hour dataset.**
   This confirms that the enhanced Stage1 pipeline is operational and can already produce a formal annual scenario package.

2. **Wind statistics are reasonably close to history.**
   The sampled wind mean is only about 1.5% above history, and wind standard deviation is about 5.4% below history.
   This indicates that the wind component is already being captured at a usable level.

3. **Wind-PV dependence is not destroyed.**
   The sampled correlation remains negative and close to the historical value, with an absolute error of about 0.021.
   This is a meaningful sign that the joint structure is at least partially preserved.

4. **The coverage metrics are not poor in a gross sense.**
   The current 90% coverage is about 0.881 for wind and 0.919 for PV.
   That means the sampled ensemble still covers a substantial portion of the historical hourly trajectory.

5. **The method is producing valid finite scenarios with explicit probabilities.**
   This is operationally important for downstream stochastic use.

#### Weaknesses

1. **PV mean is significantly underestimated.**
   The sampled PV mean is about 45.7% below the historical mean.
   This is the clearest weakness in the current enhanced Stage1 result.

2. **PV variability is also compressed.**
   The sampled PV standard deviation is about 27.9% below history, and the typical reduced scenarios compress it even further.
   This suggests that the current setup still struggles to maintain realistic PV energy volume and amplitude over the year.

3. **KMeans reduction visibly smooths the final representative scenarios.**
   The typical weighted PV standard deviation is about 55.3% below historical PV standard deviation.
   So even if the sampled ensemble contains richer variation, the reduced 10-scenario set is currently too smooth on the PV side.

4. **Ramp behavior is not yet balanced.**
   Wind ramp standard deviation becomes smaller than history, but PV ramp standard deviation becomes larger than history.
   This suggests that temporal continuity has improved in structure but not yet fully calibrated in magnitude.

At the present stage, the enhanced method can be judged as:

## 8. Future Improvement Directions

Based on the current code and results, the next improvement directions are clear.

### 8.1 Stabilize conditional covariance

Add a stricter positive-semidefinite correction for conditional covariance matrices before multivariate sampling.
This would reduce numerical fragility and help avoid unstable local samples.

### 8.2 Use a more conservative component-count strategy

The current maximum component number is fixed at 8.
For small local windows in 4D transition space, a more conservative adaptive rule may reduce local overfitting.

### 8.3 Revisit the local window size

The current `window_radius = 24` gives a one-day local neighborhood.
For 4D transition fitting, it is reasonable to compare:

- `24`
- `72`
- `168`

to test whether larger windows improve PV stability without destroying local realism.

### 8.4 Improve the reduction step

KMeans centroids are convenient, but they smooth the trajectories.
Two practical extensions are worth testing:

- centroid + nearest-real-sample retention,
- KMedoids or nearest-to-centroid representative trajectories.

This is especially relevant because current PV variability is heavily reduced after clustering.

### 8.5 Improve PV-specific modeling

The current night-PV rule is useful, but the results show that day-time PV energy is still underestimated.
Possible next steps include:

- separate treatment of day and night hours,
- month-hour conditioned PV scaling,
- seasonal normalization,
- conditional sampling with a PV-specific mixture constraint.

### 8.6 Add extreme-event protection

The current report does not yet contain a dedicated extreme-coverage diagnostic for the enhanced method.
If this Stage1 is intended to support resilience studies, then extreme low-renewable spells and high-ramp events must be checked explicitly in the next evaluation round.

### 8.7 Add downstream end-to-end validation

The current package contains Stage1 outputs only.
The most important next validation is to rerun downstream stages using the enhanced Stage1 outputs and test whether they improve dispatch realism, resilience estimation, or risk sensitivity.

## 9. Necessary Comparison Experiments for Validating This Method

To claim that the current enhanced Stage1 method is justified, the following comparison experiments are necessary.

### 9.1 Baseline A: Original Rolling DPGMM

Purpose:

- verify whether the conditional transition modeling actually improves temporal realism over the hour-wise independent version.

What it should answer:

- Does `p(x_t | x_(t-1))` reduce ramp distortion?
- Does it improve lag correlation and spell continuity?
- Does it preserve joint structure better after scenario reduction?

### 9.2 Baseline B: Historical Block Sampling / Bootstrap

Purpose:

- provide a simple time-continuity benchmark that naturally preserves local temporal structure.

What it should answer:

- Is the enhanced method doing more than rearranging history?
- Does it improve distributional flexibility while keeping realistic continuity?

### 9.3 Baseline C: Copula-Based Scenario Generation

Purpose:

- compare against a standard dependence-modeling baseline that is widely used for joint uncertainty generation.

What it should answer:

- Is the mixture-based approach more flexible than dependence modeling based mainly on copula coupling?
- Does the enhanced method better preserve nonlinear structure or temporal dependence?

### 9.4 Baseline D: VAR / ARIMA-Type Time-Series Baseline

Purpose:

- compare against classical temporal forecasting/generation models.

What it should answer:

- Is the enhanced method materially better than linear dynamics for non-Gaussian renewable data?

### 9.5 Optional Advanced Baseline: GAN / Diffusion

Purpose:

- benchmark against more expressive deep generative models if publication scope requires it.

What it should answer:

- Under the current one-year data condition, does a higher-capacity deep model actually outperform the proposed method in downstream usefulness, or does it mainly increase complexity?

This comparison is useful, but not mandatory for a first complete paper if the main contribution is the integrated resilience framework rather than a novel generative model.

## 11. Reference-Grounded Rationale

The current method choice is aligned with the literature in the following sense.

1. Scenario generation is a core tool for uncertainty propagation in renewable-rich power systems, and both generation quality and scenario reduction matter for later operational decisions.
2. Dirichlet-process mixture methods provide a flexible probabilistic modeling framework for multimodal, nonparametric density approximation.
3. Scenario reduction is a standard necessity once large sampled ensembles must be compressed for optimization.
4. Modern literature also shows that temporal dependence, multivariate dependence, and extreme-event coverage are all crucial, which means Stage1 should not be judged by marginal fit alone.

The current enhanced method fits this literature-guided design space reasonably well:

- more expressive than linear models,
- more interpretable and lighter than deep generative models,
- more continuity-aware than independent hourly sampling,
- naturally compatible with downstream scenario-based optimization.
