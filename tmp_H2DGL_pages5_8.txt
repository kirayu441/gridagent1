C. Dynamic Graph Encoding
In the previous discussion, the extraction of node features,
whether for macro or micro graphs, is confined to a specific
snapshot. However, in dynamic graphs, sequences closer to
the current time tn more accurately reflect the nodes’ current
states. Thus, we apply an exponential decay method [21]
to dynamically update the weights of node features across
different snapshots. For a snapshot arriving at time tk, its
weight at the current time tn is calculated as:
w(tk, tn) = exp(−γ (tn − tk)) (17)

IV. O FFLINE EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we conduct offline experiments on real-world
datasets from two cities provided by JD Logistics to evaluate
the performance of our proposed H2DGL framework for emer-
gency supply forecasting.
A. Datasets
We collect supply data from the JD Logistics platform for
the delivery destinations of Beijing (BJ) and Shanghai (SH),
covering the period from January 1, 2022, to August 1, 2022
(a total of 212 days). In our experiments, we consider three
types of events occurring during the different periods as the
logistics supply data, namely the pandemic, heavy rain, and
promotion, to analyze how different types of events affect
supply capabilities within the IWDSN. Based on the type of
event, the collected data is divided into three separate datasets,
named according to the destination of the supply tasks, i.e.,
Beijing and Shanghai. In each dataset, the warehouses and
sorting centers are from all over the country. The statistics of
the datasets and the event attributes used are shown in Table I,
where Lockdown Status and Categories are represented using
one-hot encoding, while the other attributes are presented as
standardized numerical sequences.
